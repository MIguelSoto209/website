The Author

I was this guy too.

Not a stylist. Not a fashion authority. A regular man who rebuilt a wardrobe that had fallen behind — and turned what worked into a repeatable system.

During the most isolated stretch of 2020, my world contracted. Almost no client contact. Only occasional contact with employees. Very few social reasons to dress up — even haircuts became a do-it-yourself job. Appearance naturally fell in priority, because daily life was asking very little of it.

As birthdays, dinners, meetings, office days, community events, photographs, and other public moments returned, the wardrobe that had been sufficient for a private life no longer provided enough range. My clothes hadn't gone wrong. Life had simply started asking more of them.

I have spent close to twenty years in insurance and business — which is exactly why I never wanted clothes to become a hobby. I wanted a system: something that told me how dressed up a moment was, what to wear for it, and what to buy next, without turning me into someone who follows menswear trends. So I built one. From Sneakers to Sharp is that system, written for the reader I was trying to become.

Book Miguel for a Podcast
The story is not “I dressed badly and fixed myself.” It is “my circumstances changed, my wardrobe stopped serving all of them, and I built a system to regain range.”

Governing Brand Principle

Respect the man. Guide the journey.

The reader is not the problem. The missing system is the problem.

Journey language, not combat language

The reader is moving forward, not fighting his former self. Comfortable clothes, sneakers, and casual habits can still have a place — the system adds range, not a new identity.

Never the punchline

The book shows underprepared → ready, not bad man → good man. When a spouse or partner buys the book, it should feel like help and relief — never criticism.

Sometimes a wardrobe does not become wrong. Life simply starts asking more of it.

For Him & For Her

A gift that feels like help, not criticism.

The ideal handoff: she recognizes the book fits his life, and he opens it feeling understood — not judged.

For him: Let your wardrobe catch up with the man you already became.
For her: Help his wardrobe catch up with the life you've built together.
